?Research Questions

Who gets to say
what a dream means?

When a classifier turns someone else's dream into a number, whose definition of "anxious" or "calm" is doing the counting — and where did that definition come from?
This project treats dream interpretation not as a puzzle to solve, but as a site where interpretive power has changed hands, again and again, without anyone noticing the handoff.

Three eras of the right to interpret

I — Shared Text

周公解梦
Zhougong's Book of Dreams

Authority lives in a public manuscript. Anyone can consult it, anyone can argue with it. Democratic — but only within one cultural frame.

II — Professional Individual

The Psychoanalyst

Authority is licensed to a trained person. The dreamer depends on the analyst to know — but at least the two of them are in the room, talking.

III — This Project

Classifier + Topic Model + Artist

Authority disperses into an opaque stack: a sentiment score, an LDA topic, a brushstroke chosen by the artist. No one to consult. No one to argue with.

Community of Practice

Who this argues with

D'Ignazio & Klein
Data Feminism — data doesn't speak for itself; counting is never neutral.
Kate Crawford
Atlas of AI — affective computing still runs on Ekman's discredited theory of universal emotion.
Lisa Nakamura
On QueerOS — critiqued for removing a subject's authority over their own experience; the same structure recurs here.
This project doesn't stand outside that critique. It is an instance of the thing being critiqued — that's the position, not an escape from it.
析Situated Technology

Built on a theory
psychology already left behind

Emotion classifiers still assume a small set of universal, legible emotions — a theory (Paul Ekman's) that has lost much of its scientific standing but never left the industry's tools.
The source material — dream-posts voluntarily published on Weibo — is also situated: a semi-confessional public genre with its own conventions of performance, not a transparent window into the unconscious.

Visual Representation

Borrowing a myth
to question it

Abstract Expressionism claimed the brushstroke reveals the artist's true inner state. This project borrows that visual language and turns it back on itself: what happens when the "artist" is an algorithm making the same claim about someone else's unconscious?
Paper tones for the shared text, cinnabar for the seal of authority, cold data-teal for the classifier — the palette itself tracks the genealogy.
算Rhetorical Argument

The claim

This project does not reveal what your dream means. It stages who gets to decide — and admits it is the newest, least accountable name on that list.
Toward a Capstone

A tentative direction

One possibility: an interactive "anti-oracle." A viewer offers a dream; the classifier, topic model, and generator run live — but the interface deliberately shows the ambiguity and the road not taken at every step, rather than delivering one confident image.
Not a solution. A gesture toward where the argument could go next.
The Challenge

Where this is still shaky

TECHNICAL
Brushwork-blend generation needs real development beyond simple style transfer.
ETHICAL

RISK
The work could become beautiful before it becomes critical — seduction outrunning the argument.
